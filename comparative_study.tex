\begin{abstract}
We implemented fifteen layer-reduction ideas for GPT-2 (124M) and
SmolLM-135M and evaluated each on \texttt{wikitext-2-raw-v1} with a fixed
perplexity protocol, repeated over three seeds with per-fit seeding. Eight
produced a usable signal; seven failed by orders of magnitude or were
skipped for stated reasons.

Three results reorganise the study away from its original hypothesis. First,
the approach that motivated this project, a fitted ternary low-rank bridge,
\emph{loses} to plain deletion while achieving identical parameter counts.
Second, deleting the lowest-influence block and repairing it with pruned
distillation works on both models, recovering 35 to 54 percent of the
perplexity that deletion costs. Third, LayerDrop fine-tuning is the strongest
single method on SmolLM-135M and is the only configuration in the whole study
that lands below its own unpruned baseline, which makes the best method
model-dependent rather than universal.

This document walks through all fifteen, gives the mathematics of the ones
that ran, and states the failure modes of the ones that did not.
\end{abstract}

\section{Setup used for every approach}
Models: GPT-2 small (124,439,808 parameters, 12 blocks) and SmolLM-135M
(134,515,008 parameters, 30 blocks), loaded with \texttt{use\_cache=False}.
Data: \texttt{Salesforce/wikitext}, config \texttt{wikitext-2-raw-v1}.
Evaluation:
\[
\mathrm{PPL}=\exp\!\Big(\tfrac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N} -\log
p_\theta(x_i \mid x_{<i})\Big)
\]
computed over non-overlapping windows, identical token budget for every
approach.

\paragraph{Seeding, and why it changed the conclusions.} Every stochastic fit
is driven by an explicit per-fit \texttt{torch.Generator}, not by a single
global seed. An earlier version of this study used a global seed and produced
figures that could not be reproduced: the same configuration yielded
$47.67/27.04$ when re-executed alone and $47.95/27.67$ when reached by running
the notebook end to end. Those figures appeared in an earlier draft of this
document and have been removed. All numbers below are the seeded ones.

\paragraph{A caution on GPT-2 and SmolLM internals.} They are not
interchangeable. GPT-2 attention builds its causal mask from
\texttt{layer\_idx}, so blocks must be re-indexed after any removal or the
model silently attends with the wrong mask. GPT-2 also stores projections as
\texttt{Conv1D} with weight shape $[\text{in},\text{out}]$, whereas SmolLM's
linear projections are $[\text{out},\text{in}]$. Code that treats both as
$\mathrm{nn.Linear}$ corrupts one of them.

\section{BI ranking, common to most approaches} \label{sec:bi}
For decoder block $l$ we record
\[
\mathrm{BI}_l \;=\; 1 - \mathbb{E}_{t}\big[\cos(h^{(l)}_t, h^{(l+1)}_t)\big]
\]
averaged over the batch and sequence. Low $\mathrm{BI}_l$ means the block is
near the identity $h^{(l+1)}\approx h^{(l)}$, so it is the natural first
candidate for deletion, merging, or aggressive quantization. GPT-2's most
redundant blocks, ascending, are $4, 1, 2, 3, 5, 6$; SmolLM's are
$2, 27, 5, 4, 3$.

\section{What we ran and how each came out}

\subsection{01 - Fitted-ternary TerGRASP bridge (RUNS, but LOSES)}
\textbf{Idea.} For each redundant block $L$, collect $X=h_{in}$,
$Y=h_{out}$ on calibration text, fit
\[
\min_{U,V}\big\| X\,U\,V - \Delta \big\|_F^2,\quad \Delta = Y-X,
\]
then AbsMean-ternarize $U,V$:
\[
\gamma=\tfrac{1}{mn}\|W\|_1,\quad
W_q=\gamma\,\mathrm{clip}\!\big(\mathrm{round}(W/\gamma),-1,1\big),
\quad W_q\in\{-\gamma,0,\gamma\}.
\]
The block is replaced by $g(x)=x+x\,U_q\,V_q$.
\textbf{Original hypothesis.} At a low-BI position $\Delta$ is small and
lives on a low-dimensional manifold, so a rank-32 factor recovers most of it.
\textbf{Result.} The hypothesis is false at this parameter budget. GPT-2
$k{=}1$: $58.67\pm9.53$ against $45.76$ for plain deletion. SmolLM $k{=}1$:
$30.77\pm3.54$ against $19.93$. Worse at every $k$.
\textbf{Why it loses.} It is strictly dominated. A rank-32 bridge is $49{,}152$
weights replacing a $7.1$M block, so bridge and deletion yield
\emph{identical} parameter counts ($117{,}351{,}936$ for both on GPT-2 at
$k{=}1$, against $124{,}439{,}808$ baseline) and indistinguishable latency
($26.54$ against $26.56$ ms). The bridge therefore deletes exactly the same
weights as deletion and then adds a perturbation on top. It can only lose.
An earlier draft of this document reported this approach as matching or
beating hard removal ($45.11$ and $21.71$). Those were the irreproducible
global-RNG figures.

\subsection{06 - ShortGPT BI baseline (WORKS, the control)}
\textbf{Idea.} Remove the $k$ lowest-BI blocks; the control removes $k$
random ones drawn \emph{disjointly} from the BI set, so the null can never
receive the block we selected.
\textbf{Result.} Disjointness matters. GPT-2: $45.76$ vs $1453.93\pm2441.72$
at $k{=}1$, $47.95$ vs $4351.09\pm203.58$ at $k{=}2$, $121.81$ vs
$2814.29\pm2378.60$ at $k{=}3$. SmolLM: $19.93$ vs $20.16\pm1.94$ at
$k{=}1$, $26.13$ vs $22417.94\pm19512.02$ at $k{=}2$, $29.91$ vs
$269.08\pm287.45$ at $k{=}3$.
\textbf{An honest qualification.} At SmolLM $k{=}1$ the two are within noise.
The claim that BI beats random \emph{by orders of magnitude at every point} is
false and we measured it. What survives is a reliability claim: BI is never
worse at any $(model,k)$, and the advantage is concentrated in the worst case
over seeds. Random removal on GPT-2 has a standard deviation exceeding its own
mean, which is exactly the property that makes a configuration undeployable.

\subsection{03 - Pruned distillation repair (WORKS, now the headline)}
\textbf{Idea.} LLM-Pruner showed depth-pruned models need distillation, so
comparing two unrepaired operators only answers which does less damage. We
repair both. The loss is
\[
\mathcal{L} = \alpha\,\mathrm{CE}(y,\hat y_S) + (1-\alpha)\,T^2\,
\mathrm{KL}\!\left(\mathrm{softmax}(\hat y_S/T)\,\middle\|\,
\mathrm{softmax}(\hat y_T/T)\right),
\]
$S$ the pruned student, $T$ the frozen original. The $T^2$ factor is not
cosmetic: softening a 50k-way distribution by $T$ scales the gradient by
$1/T^2$, so omitting it silently downweights distillation until the run is
merely fine-tuning.
\textbf{Result.} GPT-2 $k{=}1$: $45.76\rightarrow41.68\pm0.37$, recovering
$54.3\%$. GPT-2 $k{=}2$: $47.95\rightarrow44.06\pm0.17$, $40.2\%$.
SmolLM $k{=}1$: $19.93\rightarrow18.67\pm0.07$, $34.9\%$. SmolLM $k{=}2$:
$26.13\rightarrow22.14\pm0.09$, $39.4\%$. Healed beat pruned in 12 of 12 runs.
\textbf{Correction to an earlier draft.} That draft claimed the KL loss was
``essentially zero'' at a 60-step budget, so repair was a no-op. That was a
budget artefact, not a property of distillation: at 400 steps the mean initial
KL is $134.48$ falling to $34.81$, which is a very large distillation signal.
We now assert the KL is non-trivial rather than assuming it.

\subsection{11 - LayerDrop (WORKS, and better than expected on SmolLM)}
\textbf{Idea.} Fine-tune with structured layer dropout at $p=0.15$ for 500
steps, so the surviving network adapts to the removal.
\textbf{Result.} GPT-2 $k{=}1$: $51.74\pm0.13$, $k{=}2$: $57.53\pm0.28$,
both worse than plain deletion. SmolLM $k{=}1$: $\mathbf{15.54\pm0.01}$
against a $16.39$ baseline, $k{=}2$: $19.62\pm0.16$.
\textbf{Why this matters.} On SmolLM, LayerDrop lands \emph{below} the
unpruned baseline: deleting a block and fine-tuning made the model better,
with a standard deviation of $0.01$. It also beats distillation repair on the
same model ($15.54$ against $18.67$). It does not generalise, since on GPT-2
it is far worse than either deletion or repair. The best method is therefore
model-dependent, and recommending one universally would overstate the
evidence.

\subsection{10 - Learned L0 gates (RUNS, never beats BI)}
\textbf{Idea.} Train a per-block gate with an $L_0$ penalty, then delete the
lowest-gate blocks.
\textbf{Result.} GPT-2 $k{=}1$: $44.25$, $k{=}2$: $59.14$. SmolLM $k{=}1$:
$18.95$, $k{=}2$: $24.48$. Never better than the BI ranking it is meant to
improve on.
\textbf{The interesting failure.} Overlap between the learned gate ordering
and the BI ordering is exactly $0.00$ on GPT-2. The gates identify a
completely different set of blocks, most often keeping block 0 and removing
blocks 1, 2 and 5, yet arrive at a similar perplexity. Gates are a more
expensive way of rediscovering that most blocks are not equally important.

\subsection{12 - Attention-head pruning by magnitude (WORKS, clean negative)}
\textbf{Idea.} Prune the least important attention heads by magnitude.
\textbf{Result.} GPT-2: $38.25\rightarrow42.85$ at $12.5\%$ pruned,
$\rightarrow2033.08$ at $25\%$. SmolLM: $16.36\rightarrow46.92$ at $12.5\%$,
$\rightarrow102.70$ at $25\%$. Affordable at $12.5\%$ on GPT-2, catastrophic
past that, and severe immediately on SmolLM.

\subsection{07 - SVD low-rank truncation (RUNS on SmolLM)}
\textbf{Idea.} For each projection $W$ in the redundant blocks,
$W\approx U_r S_r V_r^\top$ with rank $r$.
\textbf{Maths.} By Eckart--Young the reconstruction error is the tail
$\sum_{i>r}\sigma_i^2$, small when $W$ is numerically low-rank.
\textbf{Result.} SmolLM is smooth in $r$ as expected. An earlier draft
described the GPT-2 rows as ``a dtype-guard artifact''; that was a bug we had
not isolated, and it should not have been described as noise. The mechanism
analysis in the main report supersedes this approach anyway, because it shows
local rank-truncation quality does not track end-task perplexity.

\subsection{15 - Quantized no-op bypass (RUNS, supporting rule)}
\textbf{Idea.} Quantize every weight inside the lowest-BI blocks to
$b\in\{8,4,2\}$ bits, AbsMean symmetric, and measure PPL.
\textbf{Maths.} If $F_L(x)=x+\Delta(x)$ with $\|\Delta\|$ small, the layer's
output is insensitive to bounded weight perturbations
$\|W-W_q\|_\infty\le\gamma$, so the induced output change is
$\le\|\Delta W\|\,\|x\|$, tiny next to $\|x\|$.
\textbf{Result.} SmolLM L5: $17.76$, $17.77$, $17.76$ across 8, 4 and 2
bits, i.e. essentially flat. SmolLM L2: $19.11$, $19.07$, $19.18$.
\textbf{Rule.} BI answers both questions: can this block be deleted, and can
its precision be crushed?

\subsection{13 - Early exit (DELIBERATELY SKIPPED)}
Not attempted, and we think that is correct rather than convenient. Early
exiting changes depth \emph{dynamically} per input rather than removing
blocks from the model, so it is not a layer-reduction method. It also
requires trained exit heads, which is a training project of its own.

\section{What failed, with the exact failure mode}

\subsection{02 - Affine bridge across the deleted gap}
We fit one ridge affine map $Y\approx AX+b$ from the input of the first
removed block to the output of the last and inserted it as a single
\texttt{nn.Linear}. \textbf{Why it fails.} The true map
$F_{last}\circ\dots\circ F_{first}$ is not affine; it is a composition of
LayerNorms, matmuls and nonlinearities, so one line cannot approximate it.
PPL reached $4.5\times10^3$ on GPT-2 and $1.9\times10^8$ on SmolLM.
\textbf{This was the motivation for fitting per layer, which 01 then showed
is still not enough.}

\subsection{08 - Parameter soup of adjacent blocks}
$\theta_{merge}=\alpha\theta_L+(1-\alpha)\theta_{L+1}$. \textbf{Why it
fails.} Weight-space averaging across a neuron-permutation boundary is
meaningless; GPT-2 $k{=}1$ gave $103$ to $252$ PPL against a baseline of
$38$.

\subsection{14 - Cross-layer weight tying}
Assigning $\texttt{blocks[l+d] = blocks[l]}$ shares one set of weights between
two positions. \textbf{Why it fails.} Depth is not redundant in weight space;
tying at any period $d$ exploded PPL to $10^3$ to $10^6$ times baseline.

\subsection{04 - Permutation-aligned averaging}
Capture per-neuron activations of both adjacent blocks, run Hungarian matching
to align neurons, then average. \textbf{Why it fell back.} The Hungarian step
was never hardened up and the fallback was the naive average, so the result
equals 08 and inherits its failure.

\subsection{09 - KD to a shallow student}
Wired correctly, but a shallow student changes capacity rather than depth,
so it answers a different question from the rest of this study. Reported as
partial at the budget available.

\subsection{05 - LayerDrop$\rightarrow$BI$\rightarrow$SVD$\rightarrow$KD pipeline}
Executed but produced flat rows because each stage was a scaffold rather than
a completed implementation. Retained in \texttt{archived/} as incomplete. Its
constituents are covered properly by 11, 06, 07 and 03 above.

\section{Why the headline changed}
An earlier version of this study recommended 01, the fitted ternary bridge, on
the grounds that it was the only approach that simultaneously kept graph
depth, shrank the replaced block, and held perplexity at baseline. With
per-fit seeding and error bars, all three of those claims failed: the bridge
does not hold perplexity, and it does not shrink anything, because at
rank 32 its parameter count is identical to plain deletion. Keeping graph
depth turned out to be worth nothing if the block you kept there is a worse
approximation than nothing at all.

What survives instead is a recommendation that is honest about its own scope:
delete the lowest-BI block and repair it with distillation, which is the best
method on GPT-2, and note that LayerDrop is better on SmolLM-135M and is the
only method here that improves on the unpruned baseline. The most useful
finding came from an approach that was not the point of the project.
